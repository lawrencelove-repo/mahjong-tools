A play overview for Filipino mahjong (17-tile / five-meld style). Local clubs vary; treat house rules as authoritative. Pattern references: Filipino cheatsheet.

Setup Terms Turns Hands Winning

Setup

  • Players: Four. The dealer is often called the máno (or East) and starts each hand.
  • Hand size: This is the big difference from HK/riichi — players aim for a 17-tile winning hand: five melds + one pair.
  • Tiles: Suits and honors as usual. Flowers and seasons are widely used (or blanks standing in for seasons) — match your group’s set. Agree before the first wall whether you use a dice-chosen wild (below).
  • Wall: Build four walls large enough for the fuller deal (commonly about 18 stacks × 2 when flowers/seasons are in).
  • Deal: After the dice break, 16 tiles each; the máno receives a 17th and plays first. Flowers are usually declared and replaced before the wild (if any) is chosen.

Breaking the wall with dice

Clubs vary slightly; a common pattern:

  1. The máno rolls two dice.
  2. Starting with the máno’s own wall as 1, count walls counter-clockwise to the dice total to choose the break wall. (Some tables avoid dealing from the máno’s own wall if the count lands there — they shift one wall by house rule.)
  3. From the end of that wall closest to the counter (often the end nearest the máno), count the same number of stacks and split. Many tables lift that stack and set it atop the previous one to mark a flower wall / back wall for replacements; dealing starts on the live side of the break.
  4. Deal around until everyone has 16 and the máno has 17.

Wild tile (common house rule)

Many Filipino tables do not use American-style joker tiles. Instead they pick one face from the wall to be wild for that hand only. Typical procedure:

  1. Finish the deal. Players declare flowers/seasons and take replacements until those are cleared (per your table).
  2. The máno rolls the dice again.
  3. Using that total, count that many stacks along the flower / back wall (the end used for replacements).
  4. Turn over the top tile of that stack. If it is a flower (or season), keep moving to the next stack until you reveal a suited tile (some tables also reject honors — follow the house).
  5. That revealed face is the wild for the hand: it (and usually its three matching copies in players’ hands) may stand in for other suited tiles as your club allows. Leave it face-up on the wall so everyone can see what is wild.

If your table skips wilds entirely, ignore this step. Always confirm before the first hand whether wilds are on, and whether honors can be wild.

Names & terms

Characters / circles / bamboo
The three suits. English and Tagalog/local nicknames both appear at tables.
Winds & dragons
Honors; seat and round wind may matter for bonuses depending on the club.
Flowers / seasons
Often kept as scoring or melds per house rules (sometimes treated more like honors than pure HK flower replacements). Confirm locally.
Pong / chow / kong
Triplet, sequence, quad — same building blocks as HK, but you need five sets instead of four.
Amoy / special hands
Many Filipino tables recognize a catalog of special or valued hands alongside ordinary five-meld wins; names and payouts are club-specific.
Kang / open meld
Declaring or claiming sets face-up. Chow is typically only from the prior discard.
Self-pick / throw
Win on your draw vs. on someone’s discard (wording varies by group).
Wild / joker face
Optional house rule: a dice-chosen suited tile face that can substitute for other tiles that hand (see Setup).

Turns & turn order

Play moves counter-clockwise from the dealer. A normal turn:

  1. Draw from the wall (kong replacements from the back if your table uses that convention).
  2. Resolve flowers/seasons if they are replacement tiles at your table.
  3. Win on the draw if complete; otherwise you may expose a kong or other allowed declaration.
  4. Discard one tile. Others may claim for chow (next player), pung, kong, or win, with win taking priority.

Claiming a discard for a meld means you expose, then discard, and play continues from you. Winning ends the hand immediately.

Hand formation (brief)

The ordinary win is five melds + one pair (17 tiles). Melds are chows, pongs, and kongs, open or closed. Because there is an extra meld, hands feel “longer” than HK and discard pressure differs.

Special hands (seven pairs–style variants, honor hands, flower-heavy hands, etc.) depend on the club’s list — use the cheatsheet as a starting map, then defer to local payout sheets.

Win conditions

  • Complete five melds and a pair (or a recognized special hand) and declare the win on draw or discard.
  • Tables often require a minimum value or recognize only certain special hands for large payments — ask before playing for money.
  • Payments are almost always house-ruled (fixed chips per hand type, multipliers, dealer premiums, etc.).
  • Wall exhaustion is a draw; dealer rotation follows your group’s East-stay rules.
