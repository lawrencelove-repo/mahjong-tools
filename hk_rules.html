A play overview for Hong Kong / Cantonese old-style mahjong (faan scoring). Pattern and faan lists are on the Hong Kong cheatsheet — this page covers how a hand is played.

Setup Terms Turns Hands Winning

Setup

  • Players: Four. East is dealer; winds rotate as the game progresses through rounds.
  • Tiles: Usually 136 (suits + honors). Flowers and seasons (8+8) are common house options — include them, exclude them, or substitute blanks. Agree before dealing; the cheatsheet’s season setting mirrors that choice for examples.
  • No jokers in standard HK play.
  • Wall: Four walls of 17×2 (or 18×2 if flowers/seasons are in). Dice set the break point.
  • Deal: 13 tiles each; East takes a 14th and starts. If flowers/seasons are used, players often replace them immediately from the back of the wall until the dealt hand has none (table rule).

Names & terms

Characters / circles / bamboo
The three suits (man / pin / sou).
Winds & dragons
Honors. Seat wind and prevailing (round) wind matter for scoring.
Flowers & seasons
Bonus tiles when included. Drawing one usually scores and replaces from the wall; they are not kept as part of the 13-tile structure in the same way as suits.
Pong / chow / kong
Triplet, sequence (chow only from the previous player’s discard), and quad. English tables often say pung/chi/kong.
Faan (fan)
Scoring units. Tables set a minimum faan to win (commonly 3). Patterns add faan; payments scale with total faan.
Self-draw / discard win
Win on your draw vs. claiming someone’s discard (often called “joker-free” ron/tsumo equivalents — HK speech varies).
Ready / listening
One tile from winning (tenpai). Some tables care at a draw; many HK casual games simply end with a washout.

Turns & turn order

Order is counter-clockwise from East. On your turn:

  1. Draw one tile from the front of the wall (kong replacements come from the back).
  2. If flowers/seasons are in play and you drew one, set it aside for scoring and replace until you have a normal tile (per table).
  3. You may win on the draw, declare a kong, or otherwise act per house rules.
  4. Discard one tile. Others may claim it for a chow (only the next player), pung, kong, or win — with win usually beating other claims.

After a claimed meld, the claimant discards and play continues from them. A win ends the hand.

Hand formation (brief)

A standard winning hand is four sets + one pair (14 tiles). Sets are chows, pongs, or kongs, open or closed. Special hands (seven pairs, thirteen orphans, and various limited hands) appear on the faan chart when your table recognizes them.

Unlike riichi, you generally do not need a named “yaku” beyond meeting the table’s minimum faan — patterns themselves supply the faan.

Win conditions

  • Complete a legal hand that meets the table’s minimum faan.
  • Win on self-draw or on a discard (unless a house rule forbids certain discards).
  • Score by totaling faan from patterns, then apply the table’s payment scheme (often loser(s) pay the winner; self-draw may mean all three pay).
  • If the wall runs out: draw / washout; dealer may stay or rotate by agreement.
