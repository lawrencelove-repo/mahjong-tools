A play overview for National Mah Jongg League (NMJL) / American mahjong. Hands come from the current year’s card — see the American cheatsheet for pattern families, not a substitute for an official card.

Setup Terms Charleston Turns Hands Winning

Setup

  • Players: Four. East is dealer for the hand; seats may be called by wind or simply East/South/West/North around the table.
  • Tiles: 152 — the usual 136 suited and honor tiles, plus 8 flowers and 8 jokers. One white dragon is treated as soap (zero) for number hands. Leave out unused blanks if your set has extras.
  • Wall: Build four walls (typically 19 stacks × 2 high).
  • Deal: After the dice break, each player receives 13 tiles; East takes a 14th. Then run the Charleston before anyone discards.
  • Card: Every player needs the current NMJL card. Legal winning hands are only those printed on the card (plus any league clarifications).

Breaking the wall with dice

  1. East rolls two dice.
  2. Starting with East’s wall as 1, count walls around the table (usually counter-clockwise) to the dice total to choose which wall to break.
  3. From one end of that wall (club convention: often the right end as that player faces it), count the same number of stacks and split there.
  4. Deal from the break around the table until everyone has 13 and East has 14. Exact batching (fours vs. twos) is house style; the dice break is what matters for fairness.

Names & terms

Craks / bams / dots
The three suits (characters, bamboo, circles). Numbers 1–9.
Dragons & winds
Honors. White dragon is often soap (0) in number hands; green and red are dragons. Winds are E/S/W/N.
Flowers
Eight flower tiles. They are not a suit; card hands specify when flowers are used (often as a group or with jokers per card text).
Jokers
Wild tiles that can stand in for most tiles in exposures and concealed hands, with card-specific limits (e.g. never in a singlet or certain pairs — follow the card).
Exposure
An open meld claimed from a discard (or sometimes self-called), displayed face-up. American play talks about exposures more than chi/pon vocabulary.
Pung / kong / quint
Triplet, quad, and five-of-a-kind (jokers make quints possible). Sequences are “runs” or named by the card.
Soap
White dragon used as zero in hands that need 0s (e.g. 2024-style number hands on some cards).
Mahjong
Calling a win when your 14th tile completes a card hand.
Dead hand
If you make an illegal exposure or otherwise break the rules for your declared line, your hand can be declared dead — you stop playing that hand.

Charleston (passing)

Before the first discard, players exchange tiles in a fixed ritual. Passes are simultaneous; you must pass the required number of tiles you still hold (never look at what you receive until your pass is out).

  1. First Charleston — right: Pass 3 tiles to the player on your right; receive 3 from your left.
  2. First Charleston — across: Pass 3 across the table; receive 3 from opposite.
  3. First Charleston — left: Pass 3 to your left; receive 3 from your right.
  4. Optional second Charleston: By agreement (often after a vote or “do you want a second?”), repeat right / across / left with 3 tiles each, or skip.
  5. Courtesy pass: Optional last exchange — typically up to 3 tiles across with the player opposite (you may pass fewer, including zero, if both agree on the count).

Jokers may be passed. After the Charleston (and courtesy), East discards to begin the hand. There is no further mandatory passing mid-hand.

Turns & turn order

After Charleston, East discards first. Play continues counter-clockwise (to the right of the player who just discarded, in common American seating talk — follow your table’s “next player” convention consistently).

  1. On your turn, pick the top tile from the wall (unless you just claimed a discard for an exposure — then you usually expose and discard without picking).
  2. You may declare mahjong if the pick completes a card hand.
  3. Otherwise discard one tile face-up.
  4. Others may call that discard to complete an exposure allowed by the hand they are building, or to mahjong. Calls interrupt the next pick; the claiming player exposes and discards (or wins).

Exact call priority and whether you can claim for concealed-only hands vary slightly by club; when in doubt, NMJL card footnotes and league rulings control.

Hand formation overview

You are always aiming at a specific line on the card (sometimes switching lines as tiles arrive). Patterns are groups of pungs, kongs, pairs, singles, and runs as printed — not free-form “any four melds + pair.”

Jokers substitute inside many groups; flowers and soap appear only where the card shows them. Exposures must match a legal interpretation of your hand; illegal exposures can kill the hand.

Win conditions

  • Complete a hand exactly as shown on the current card (concealed or with the exposures the card allows).
  • Call mahjong on a self-pick or on a discard that finishes the hand.
  • Payoffs follow the card’s value (and any table multipliers). Hot wall / wall game (no one wins) is a push or table-specific settlement.
